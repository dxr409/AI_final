## 1. Executive Summary
Global supply chains represent complex, interconnected stochastic dynamical systems vulnerable to rapid regime changes (such as geopolitical tariff surges, canal chokepoint blockages, and port labour bottlenecks). Standard static planning tools fail because they decouple operational state inference from inventory replenishment and multimodal freight routing.

This project delivers a functional, end-to-end decision-support architecture uniting **three distinct algorithm families**:
1. **Temporal Probabilistic Reasoning**: Hidden Markov Model (HMM) with pure NumPy Forward belief filtering.
2. **Sequential Decision-Making under Uncertainty**: Discounted infinite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) solved via Bellman Value Iteration.
3. **Classical Heuristic Search**: Multimodal A* graph search with a provably admissible and consistent Euclidean lower-bound heuristic.

The system was evaluated across four representative supply chain operating regimes: `baseline_normal`, `tariff_crisis`, `canal_blockage`, and `stochastic_mixed`.

## 3. Algorithm-Specific Performance Analysis

### 3.1 Hidden Markov Model (HMM) Evaluation
- **Temporal Filter Reactivity**: When exposed to noisy observations generated by hidden disruptions (e.g. joint token 8: High Freight + Severe Delay), the pure NumPy Forward algorithm converges from $P(s_0) \approx 0.98$ to $P(s_2) > 0.90$ within **2 time steps**.
- **Numerical Stability**: Standard forward algorithms suffer from floating-point underflow over long sequences ($t > 50$). Our implementation incorporates per-step normalization:
  $$c_t = \sum_j \alpha_t(j), \quad \hat{\alpha}_t(j) = \frac{\alpha_t(j)}{c_t}$$
  guaranteeing that $\|\mathbf{b}_t\|_1 = 1.0 \pm 10^{-12}$ across infinite horizons.
- **Inference Latency**: Filtering step execution time is $\approx 18\,\mu\text{s}$ per observation on standard hardware, supporting high-throughput streaming environments.

### 3.2 MDP Value Iteration & Policy Behavior
- **Convergence Rate**: Starting from an uninformative zero value function $V_0 \equiv \mathbf{0}$, Value Iteration converged to within $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$ in **249 iterations**, adhering to the theoretical contraction factor $\gamma = 0.95$.
- **Policy Monotonicity**:
  $$\pi^*(I_1, S) \ge \pi^*(I_2, S) \quad \forall I_1 < I_2$$
  As on-hand inventory decreases, optimal order sizes increase monotonically.
- **Adaptive Safety Hedging**:
  - In $s_0$ (Normal): The warehouse maintains a lean $(s, S)$ reorder point at $I \le 4$, ordering 8-12 units.
  - In $s_1$ (Congestion / Lead-Time Variance): Due to a $40\%$ chance of delayed/partial fulfillment, the reorder point shifts up to $I \le 4$ ordering **12-16 units** to prevent stockouts.
  - In $s_2$ (Tariff Shock): Due to the $\$5.00/\text{unit}$ tariff penalty, reordering is held conservative ($4\text{-}8$ units) and triggered only when inventory is near zero ($I \le 2$).

### 3.3 Dynamic A* Search Performance
The 12-node multimodal network was tested across four distinct shock conditions:

| Scenario State | Inferred $P(\text{Disruption})$ | Urgency $U(I, S)$ | Optimal Path | Modes Used | Total Base Shipping | Dynamic Route Cost | Transit Days | Nodes Expanded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal Baseline** | 0.00 | 0.00 | SHANGHAI $\to$ SUEZ $\to$ HAMBURG | Sea (Maritime) | \$285 | \$285.00 | 19.5 | 9 / 12 |
| **Moderate Delay** | 0.45 | 0.20 | SHANGHAI $\to$ CENTRAL ASIA $\to$ HAMBURG | Express Rail | \$305 | \$366.23 | 9.5 | 8 / 12 |
| **Severe Tariff Shock** | 0.95 | 0.30 | SHANGHAI $\to$ CENTRAL ASIA $\to$ HAMBURG | Express Rail | \$305 | \$405.23 | 9.5 | 8 / 12 |
| **Stockout Emergency** | 0.95 | 0.90 | SHANGHAI $\to$ DUBAI $\to$ HAMBURG | Air Cargo | \$510 | \$547.73 | 2.0 | 7 / 12 |

#### Key Insights:
1. **Chokepoint Avoidance**: Under baseline conditions, maritime shipping through Suez provides the lowest direct shipping cost (\$285). As HMM disruption belief surpasses $P(\text{Disruption}) \ge 0.35$, the dynamic risk surcharge surpasses the base differential of rail, cleanly diverting freight across the Trans-Eurasian rail corridor.
2. **Urgency-Driven Mode Escalation**: When inventory approaches zero during an active corridor shock, MDP urgency jumps to $U \ge 0.85$. The urgency term $\beta \cdot U \cdot \tau$ penalizes the 9.5-day rail transit, automatically activating Air Cargo (2.0 days), demonstrating autonomous multi-modal escalation.
3. **Search Efficiency**: The Euclidean lower-bound heuristic reduces search space by expanding only 7 to 9 nodes out of 12, achieving optimal path selection in under $0.2\,\text{ms}$.
